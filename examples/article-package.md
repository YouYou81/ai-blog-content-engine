## Introduction

An AI blog content engine reduces SEO production cost by turning repeatable work into a measurable workflow. The system starts with a Google Sheet keyword pool, moves each topic through outline, draft, optimization, image generation, and publishing, then tracks search performance after release. This approach does not remove editorial judgment. It gives editors better inputs, clearer quality gates, and faster iteration. Teams can use it to produce technical blogs, glossary pages, product education content, comparison pages, and FAQ-led articles with a consistent operating rhythm.

## Why Google Sheet Works As The Keyword Pool

Google Sheet is simple enough for content operators and structured enough for automation. Each row can represent one content asset with fields for keyword, intent, priority, owner, status, platform, URL, and performance metrics.

## What The Multi-Stage Pipeline Controls

The pipeline separates content production into stages:

| Stage | Output | Quality Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Topic selection | Approved keyword and angle | Business relevance |
| Outline | H2/H3 structure | Intent coverage |
| First draft | Full article package | Completeness |
| Optimization | Improved article | SEO and E-E-A-T score |
| Image generation | 16:9 cover | Brand fit |
| Publishing | CMS draft or live URL | Operational readiness |
| Tracking | GSC metrics | Template effectiveness |

## How SEO Quality Checks Help

Automated checks reduce repetitive review work. The system can score readability, estimate keyword density, check metadata length, flag duplicate topics, review internal and external links, and evaluate whether the article demonstrates E-E-A-T signals.

## How Publishing And Tracking Close The Loop

After the article is drafted, the publisher creates a draft in WordPress, Ghost, or Notion. The URL is written back to Google Sheet. Later, Google Search Console data is pulled into the same row so the team can compare impressions, clicks, CTR, and average position across templates.

## FAQ

### Can this workflow publish to multiple platforms?

Yes. The default adapters are WordPress, Ghost, and Notion, but the same pattern can work with any CMS API.

### Why use Google Sheet instead of a database?

Google Sheet is easier for content operators to use. A database can replace it later when the workflow needs stricter permissions or larger scale.

### Does the system automatically publish live articles?

It can, but draft-first publishing is safer. Most teams should create drafts, run review, then publish.

### How does GSC data improve future content?

GSC data shows which queries, titles, templates, and topic clusters perform best. That evidence can update prompts and prioritize new keywords.
